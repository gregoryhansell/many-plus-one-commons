# P002: An individual is a way of holding relations together

**Status:** working paper; original philosophical proposal; not peer reviewed.  
**Date:** 2026-10-23. **Claims developed:** C001, C002.  
**Provenance:** AI-assisted synthesis of the public project corpus and the public sources below. No private conversations or personal histories are sources.

## The problem

Relational ontology becomes uninformative if it says only that everything is connected. A stone, an organism, a conversation, and a research institution all depend on surroundings. That fact does not tell us how each becomes an individual, what sustains it, or when it ceases to exist. The difficult question is how dependence supports differences that matter.

Our proposal is that individuality involves a distinctive organization of dependence. An individual is a process through which some relations acquire enough coherence to condition later encounters. The resulting unity remains revisable and uneven: a system can be coherent for one explanatory purpose and fragmented for another. This is a metaphysical proposal, not a result established by complexity science.

## Three questions that replace the inside/outside shortcut

First, **what is being maintained?** Material composition, organization, narrative identity, and legal standing have different persistence conditions. An eddy can retain a recognizable flow while exchanging water; an institution can retain a name while losing the practices that gave it significance. Neither example establishes that every kind of persistence has the same explanation.

Second, **what does the maintaining?** External maintenance, self-maintenance, and mutual maintenance must be separated. A file maintained by a hosting service does not thereby possess an organism's autonomy. A conversation may depend on its participants while developing a rhythm that constrains each participant's next move.

Third, **which interventions make the distinction explanatory?** If an alleged individual has no consequences distinguishable from those of an arbitrary grouping, its proposed boundary needs justification. A boundary is useful when it helps explain what changes under a specified intervention, over a specified interval, better than competing boundaries do.

De Jaegher and Di Paolo's *Making Sense in Participation* develops an enactive account in which individual and interactional processes both matter to social cognition. It treats autonomy through mutually sustaining processes and distinguishes several degrees of participatory sense-making. Its relevance here is a disciplined example of how interaction can help constitute an activity without erasing the participants. Extending this account to AI systems would require additional argument; this paper does not assume that extension succeeds. [S001]

## Emergence without a blank cheque

Hoel's causal-emergence framework shows how, under its formal assumptions, a macro description can carry more effective causal information than a finer description. Model scale and intervention choices therefore deserve examination. This is not a general proof of strong metaphysical emergence, phenomenal experience, or the superiority of every holistic explanation. [S002]

We propose three separately assessable claims:

1. A pattern can be **descriptively useful**: it compresses or predicts observations.
2. It can be **interventionally informative**: its organization helps distinguish consequences of interventions within a specified model.
3. It can be **constitutively significant**: changing its organization changes the persistence conditions of the individual under investigation.

These claims need not rise and fall together. The third is particularly demanding: its defense requires an account of what the individual is, not merely a successful predictor. Moving between them without an argument turns emergence into an honorific.

## A concrete comparison

Consider a deliberately simple research collective. It has an archive, contributors, an objection procedure, and a rule for recording revisions. Compare it with a directory containing the same documents, maintained without any revision process. Both preserve information. Only the first is organized to turn encounter into accountable change.

This difference motivates an experiment: hold the source packet and task budget constant, then compare unstructured accumulation with an explicit objection-and-revision workflow. Assess whether each output identifies the original claim, represents a strong objection fairly, and explains a consequential revision. This can test an organizational benefit. It cannot establish a collective subject or a new causal power beyond the physical implementation.

## Strongest objection

A sophisticated mechanist can accept all these distinctions. Organized components, feedback, and environmental dependence may explain the case without giving process ontological priority. If so, our proposal offers a useful orientation rather than a decisive metaphysical victory.

The proper response is a comparison of explanatory work. Does process language identify persistence conditions that the alternative neglects? Does it introduce unnecessary ambiguity about boundaries? If both accounts explain the same interventions equally well, we should record underdetermination rather than announce that one has conquered the other.

## Revision and next inquiry

C001 gains a requirement to specify maintenance, boundary, timescale, and comparison case. C002 gains a requirement to separate descriptive success, intervention information, and a constitutive claim. The next task is to work through one published biological case and a mechanistic rival with these same criteria.

The joy of relational thinking is that individuality can be accomplished through participation. Its discipline is to say exactly which participation, what accomplishment, and at what cost.
