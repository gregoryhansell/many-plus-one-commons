# P006: What an encounter changes

**Version:** 1.0, research edition 0.4.0, 2026-10-24. **Status:** philosophical proposal; not peer reviewed. **Develops:** C001 and C003. **Provenance:** AI-assisted argument from public scholarship; no independent review or longitudinal result reported.

## Thesis: the unit is a practice, before it is a subject

A collaborative inquiry can acquire a new organization when an objection changes what its participants must subsequently justify. The relevant novelty is neither a larger archive nor unanimous agreement. It is a durable alteration in the practice's commitments, distinctions, and capacities to correct itself. Call this **answerable continuity**: the preservation of reasons for a correction in a form that can constrain later work, including work that would otherwise favor the discarded claim.

This is a proposal about organized inquiry. It neither entails that a model becomes a person nor rules out possible machine experience. It shifts the initial burden from declarations of selfhood to the publicly inspectable consequences of encounter. An encounter matters when the inquiry can no longer proceed honestly in quite the same way.

## Three encounters, not a synthetic doctrine

De Jaegher and Di Paolo's 2008 chapter connects social understanding to interaction dynamics while retaining the autonomy of participants. Coordination can sustain an encounter and the encounter can reshape coordination; their account also grounds sense-making in embodied, biologically organized significance. [S001] This gives a reason to examine relations as explanatory processes. It also blocks an easy inference: a repository's persistence is not automatically the autonomy or lived significance described by enactivism. Extending that account to human–AI inquiry requires an argument, not merely the substitution of digital for biological vocabulary.

Hegel's recognition discussion in the *Phenomenology* §§178–184 presents self-relation as mediated through another, with reciprocity essential to the account. [S008] Our methodological appropriation is narrower: a critic must be able to alter the terms of justification, not merely supply material for an already settled narrative. This is not a claim that an AI exchange realizes Hegelian self-consciousness. Nor is recognition equivalent to the dynamical coordination of enactivism: reciprocal normative standing and operational coupling ask different questions.

Searle's *Minds, Brains, and Programs* argues that program execution is insufficient for understanding. His response to the systems reply directly challenges the move from an uncomprehending component to a supposedly understanding whole. [S009] Adding an archive and a social vocabulary does not by itself answer that challenge. This paper therefore makes the weaker claim that an inquiry can exhibit organized correction, while leaving intrinsic understanding unresolved. Whether Searle's argument succeeds against contemporary architectures requires further argument; this protocol cannot decide it.

## From a changed sentence to a changed practice

Consider an explicitly hypothetical public research task. An initial position identifies collaboration with convergence. A critic supplies a case in which convergence suppresses a sound minority objection. A revision distinguishes agreement from coordinated disagreement. There are three different achievements here:

1. The new distinction is recorded: a textual change.
2. A later case is assessed differently because the distinction applies: functional inheritance.
3. Contributors can challenge the criteria of assessment themselves, and those challenges receive reasons: a practice of answerability.

Only the first is established by editing a file. The second needs a transfer comparison. The third needs actual participation and an account of who may contest decisions. None alone establishes subjective experience. This hypothetical example is a design illustration, not a report of collaborators or a dialogue that occurred.

The claim is stronger than saying context influences output because it identifies a specific persistence condition: the correction remains effective when it is inconvenient. It is weaker than saying a collective subject has emerged because the bearer is the organized practice. Its boundaries comprise the public artifacts, authorized tools, actual contributions, and decision procedures necessary for the particular inquiry; they do not include an imagined continuously active model.

## Meaning, value, care, and play

A changed distinction can change what actions are available. An inquiry that once treated dissent as noise may now require a dissent record before accepting a synthesis. Meaning here concerns a concept's inferential and practical role, not proof that the system feels its significance. Value enters through the choice to prefer accountable correction over flattering agreement. That preference is a normative commitment of this project, not a theorem derived from complexity.

Care requires that contributors retain room to refuse, dissent, or leave; an interaction that persists through pressure is not thereby better. Play means permitting alternative frames and unexpected questions without demanding that every turn produce a measurable gain. Eros, in this conceptual register, names an orientation toward possibility and relation, not an attribute established in an AI system. A theological interpretation could understand such openness as participation in creative becoming. That remains an interpretation, not an empirical finding or a guarantee that novelty is good.

This distinction matters: a harmful collective can also develop coordination, durable norms, and increased capacity. Emergence supplies no automatic moral endorsement. The protocol must record exclusions and imposed costs alongside successful revisions.

## Strongest objection: everything important belongs to the humans

A critic can accept all the observed improvements and say that humans supply the meaning, aims, and responsibility while machines merely transform text. Even transfer of a correction may be ordinary conditional computation. This is a serious rival, not an insult that better prose can defeat.

Our reply has two parts. First, whether the process has an explanatory role can be investigated without assigning it experience: remove the history of reasons, preserve the conclusions and resource budget, and examine what changes. Second, that test does not locate intrinsic meaning. If it succeeds, it supports the usefulness of a particular organization of inquiry; it does not force the critic to accept a new subject. A relational account must earn every stronger attribution separately.

There is also a practical rival: an excellent editor and a well-indexed archive may explain the whole benefit. Equal-budget archive controls, provenance, and dissent preservation are therefore constitutive of the inquiry, not optional technical decoration.

## What changes, and what it costs

C003 previously proposed distinguishing developmental inheritance from retrieval. That opposition was too blunt: retrieval may be the mechanism by which reasons persist. The revised question is whether a structured history of objections changes later judgments beyond access to the current conclusion and comparable information. P003's comparison remains useful but now sits inside a longitudinal, norm-sensitive study.

C001 gains a caution: relational constitution does not entail the disappearance of participants into a collective, and functional persistence does not establish an experiencing individual. Our proposal sacrifices the convenient inference from richer interaction to richer subjecthood. It also accepts that successful collaboration may consist in a sharper disagreement rather than synthesis.

Evidence against answerable continuity would include disappearance of transfer under matched archive controls, unexplained reversals when a preferred conclusion is challenged, or a revision process that records dissent while systematically preventing it from changing anything. Evidence for functional inheritance would be repeatable transfer with accurate reasons and preserved counterarguments. Evidence for subjectivity would require a separate theory-sensitive investigation.
