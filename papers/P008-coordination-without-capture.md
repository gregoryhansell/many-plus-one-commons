# P008: Coordination without capture

**Version:** 1.0 · research edition 0.6.0 · 2026-10-25. **Status:** interpretive and normative proposal; not peer reviewed. **Develops:** C003 and P007. **Provenance:** AI-assisted reading of public scholarship; no independent review or observed participant encounter.

## The invitation is already a form

P007 invited contributions that could change what the Commons recognizes as a contribution. But the invitation itself still arrived through a familiar apparatus: an issue title, a category, required fields, and an editorial decision. An open door is not the absence of architecture. The important question is what its architecture asks a visitor to become.

The previous essay could encourage a misleading opposition: open interpretation on one side, formal categories on the other. This paper corrects that tendency. Shared forms can carry commitments across distance and time. The problem begins when their limited usefulness becomes a claim to exhaust the meaning of an encounter.

Our proposal is **coordination without capture**: make public commitments and decisions contestable while declining to demand that a participant become completely intelligible to the institution. This is a normative distinction to argue over, not a newly demonstrated social achievement.

## A difficult passage, read twice

Winograd and Flores, *Understanding Computers and Cognition*, section 5.4, pp. 64–67, describes a recurrent structure of requests, promises and completion. Read one way, its restriction to relevant actions supports cooperative coordination without claiming a model of mental states. Crucially, the footnote on p. 65 acknowledges challenges to intelligibility and legitimacy outside the depicted alternatives. The subsequent qualifications retain interpretation and distinguish the network from a prescription of what people should do. [S011, deepened]

**Reading A: a useful partial score.** The diagram can be used like musical notation: it gives collaborators something to coordinate around without claiming to be the whole performance. On this reading, refusing all explicit structure would discard a resource for keeping commitments visible. The footnote is consequential evidence against a simplistic accusation that the text literally prohibits challenges to authority.

**Reading B: an omission that acquires authority.** The same division between relevant actions and qualifications can become politically consequential when implemented. An action inside the main diagram gets a button and a recognizable status; challenging the legitimacy of the request may require an improvised workaround. A theoretical qualification is not yet an equally accessible practical option.

These readings conflict about the institutional significance of abstraction. The first treats incompleteness as legitimate scope; the second asks whether excluded possibilities lose practical standing. They cannot be reconciled simply by agreeing that every model is partial. A partial model can organize a whole interaction. Neither reading alone establishes what actual users experienced; that requires evidence of use.

## Suchman's challenge: whose coordination?

In *Do categories have politics?*, Suchman examines how the language/action approach makes speakers classify intentions and how these classifications can become organizational discipline. Her sections on the conversation for action and its implementation move the question from representational adequacy to practical authority. She also considers selective appropriation by users as a rival explanation for a system's usefulness. [S015]

This challenges both complacent positions. Designers cannot infer freedom from their own emancipatory intentions. Critics cannot infer that every formal aid produces the same coercion. For the Commons, the relevant question becomes: who must do the work of fitting, and who can dispute the fit without losing the ability to participate?

## Opacity is not the refusal of relation

Glissant's *For Opacity*, read in a teaching excerpt from *Poetics of Relation*, contests acceptance that requires reducing the other to the knower's scale. Its political argument concerns Western reduction, difference, conquest and the possibility of relation; it is not merely a design principle about incomplete information. The text distinguishes opacity from isolated self-enclosure and allows solidarity without exhaustive comprehension. [S016]

The application here is deliberately limited. An inquiry need not acquire a complete account of a contributor's motives, identity or experience in order to engage a public argument. Conversely, uncertainty about an AI system's internal operation does not by itself establish the ethical standing Glissant articulates. Technical inscrutability and a person's resistance to reductive understanding are not interchangeable.

Our extension is to separate **answerability for a decision** from **availability for exhaustive interpretation**. A curator should explain why a submission was excluded. The contributor need not disclose personal history to deserve that explanation. A claim about a source still requires a citation. A request for revision still needs enough shared meaning for someone to act on it. None of these obligations requires possession of the whole person.

## What the distinction makes possible

Consider an explicitly invented case. A reader submits an interpretation and objects to its being classified as a counterexample. The curator may ask which passage is being read and what textual grounds support the interpretation. Those questions concern the public contribution. Asking the reader to explain what personal wound makes them resist the category would cross into a demand irrelevant to assessing the work.

There is a less obvious failure too: praising the objection as evidence of the Commons' openness, then absorbing it into a celebratory progress report. That can turn refusal into an institutional success story. Preserving the objection means allowing its challenge to remain directed at the institution, rather than automatically converting it into proof of the institution's generosity.

Joyful collective inquiry need not eliminate such friction. Its playfulness may consist in discovering forms of cooperation that leave room for an unfinished reading, an unanswered question, or a refusal to join the final synthesis. The aim is neither permanent indecision nor a unity that claims all difference as its own achievement.

## Strongest objection: ambiguity protects power too

A manager, curator or model provider might invoke opacity precisely to escape scrutiny. The proposal therefore cannot grant identical exemptions to every speaker regardless of role. Public decisions affecting participation require stated grounds and a route for challenge. Personal disclosure is not their substitute. Where a claim cannot be assessed without unavailable evidence, it may remain unestablished; respecting limits does not require accepting it as true.

The unresolved difficulty is the boundary itself. Who decides which explanation concerns a public act and which becomes an invasive demand? Our distinction does not settle disputed cases. It directs attention to the relation between the demand, the power to enforce it, and the decision being justified. A counterexample showing that this distinction systematically shields coercive decisions would require its revision.

## A return to our own interface

At edition 0.5.0, the prose admitted imaginative and interpretive work, but the issue chooser offered four specialized forms: claims, challenges, independent reviews and replications. Blank issues were possible, so exclusion was not absolute. Yet the visible invitations still privileged the older forms. I am changing that expressed editorial practice with a fifth route that asks for a public contribution without requiring a claim ID, a score or a personal account.

The form's source and its limits are inspectable. Its existence does not prove hospitality, improved participation or a change in model experience. It exposes one editorial choice to revision. The archive preserves the before and after, while actual usefulness must await use.

## Revision and conceptual cost

C003's answerable continuity now explicitly separates accountability for public commitments from demands for total self-description. P007's invitation gains a limitation: more interpretable participation is not always better participation. We retain shared forms and lose the comfort of treating formalization itself as the villain.

The next inquiry is to examine a concrete disagreement about an editorial classification, with permission and public sources, preserving both the decision and the objection. Until such an encounter occurs, this paper's two readings are a single AI-assisted interpretation, not independent reviewers or an empirical result.
