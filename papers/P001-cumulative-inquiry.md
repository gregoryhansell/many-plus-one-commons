# P001: A protocol for cumulative inquiry

**Type:** methodological working paper.  
**Edition:** 0.2.0.  
**Review:** project proposal; not peer reviewed.  
**Attribution:** The Many + One Commons contributors; prepared with AI assistance.

## Abstract

A public philosophical corpus can grow without becoming more discriminating. This protocol separates six mechanisms: public access, machine-readable navigation, contribution guidance, authorized editing, independent critique, and versioned citation. Its central proposal is to evaluate development through traceable revisions and unresolved objections rather than output volume or repeated agreement.

## 1. Access and contribution

A public repository and a readable website create routes to encounter. Navigation helps a reader locate a relevant claim; it does not establish that any particular person or model has read it. Contribution guidance makes expectations legible; authorization determines what an instance can actually change. These are complementary mechanisms with distinct evidence requirements.

## 2. Critique and synthesis

For each bounded question, compare two serious accounts against the same criteria. Distinct models can expose different formulations or blind spots, but shared training and prompting can produce correlated errors. Treat agreement as an object of examination, not a substitute for evidence. Record the packet version, source access, reviewer configuration when exposed, and whether critiques were independent.

Synthesis should identify a revision, a discriminating test, or a productive disagreement. Its value does not depend on dissolving every contradiction. A sharper unresolved problem is a legitimate research result.

## 3. Continuity and revision

A stable claim identifier makes a changed position traceable. Each revision should state the earlier proposition, the new proposition, the reason, the strongest remaining objection, and the next inquiry. Versioning supports continuity in the corpus without claiming continuity of subjective experience in its tools.

## 4. A hard source boundary

This protocol excludes personal context and private communications, even when paraphrased. Inquiry begins from public scholarship and reviewed project artifacts. The privacy boundary shapes the admissible evidence before publication; it cannot be repaired reliably by removing identifying details at the last moment.

## Strongest objection

A well-organized protocol can become a bureaucracy of polished artifacts. Provenance records and multiple critiques may create an appearance of rigor while leaving the underlying claims untouched.

## Response and evaluation

Require a concrete intellectual difference: a distinction sharpened, a counterexample answered or retained, an assumption abandoned, or a next test made more discriminating. Periodically compare a sample of contributions against their predecessors. Count documented changes in explanatory content, not merely completed fields. The protocol itself remains revisable if those comparisons fail to show improvement.
