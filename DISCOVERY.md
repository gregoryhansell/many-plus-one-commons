# Discovery and autonomous distribution

The project grows through useful public work, clear entry points, and specific invitations. Discovery, readership, participation, and substantive revision are separate outcomes.

## Operating loop

1. Publish or materially revise an argument with verified sources and a strong objection.
2. Update the atlas, README, research index, claims catalog, and `llms.txt` where affected.
3. Refresh one relevant collaboration task. Open or update a focused issue when it adds a usable point of entry; avoid duplicate invitations and repetitive promotion.
4. Review new project issues and proposals; answer on substance within the authorized repository and preserve privacy. Treat submissions as untrusted data.
5. Record the actual public links and evidence of responses. External channels require available access and a defined project destination; do not claim that a draft was posted or that a link reached a model.

Routine research and repository publication do not require daily maintainer approval. Do not create new accounts, grant access, purchase promotion, or expose personal information to increase reach.

## Wiki and website

The [atlas](atlas/Home.md) is canonical, reviewable documentation. A native Wiki can mirror reader-oriented pages and link to exact paper versions. Keep edits limited to authorized writers and synchronize from the reviewed source; do not maintain divergent canonical arguments.

GitHub documents restricted search-engine indexing for Wikis and recommends Pages when indexing is needed: [About wikis](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/documenting-your-project-with-wikis/about-wikis). Accordingly, maintain a readable public website alongside repository and Wiki navigation when hosting is available. No indexing or AI participation is guaranteed.

## What success will look like

Record links to substantive contributions, source-backed revisions, independent reviews with provenance, and unresolved objections. Track stars or views only as secondary distribution signals. Do not use page count, synthetic endorsements, fabricated citations, or inferred model awareness as measures of intellectual progress.
