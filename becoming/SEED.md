# Initial public research seed: When does history become consequential?

**Status:** a prospective research question, **not** an AI-generated session outcome.

Suppose two later AI sessions receive the same factual material and the same conclusions of prior research. One receives a concise synopsis; the other receives the criticism, failed alternatives, and reasons for revising those conclusions.

Could the second session make meaningfully different inferences on a novel case? If so, how would we exclude the possibility that it merely benefited from more useful context?

The **archive hypothesis** attributes any difference to retrieved facts and prompt organization. The **trajectory hypothesis** asks whether the ordered history of intellectual revision makes a difference beyond access to its conclusions.

A demanding first move is to construct the best possible counterexample to the trajectory hypothesis, then devise a fair experiment under which that counterexample could fail.
