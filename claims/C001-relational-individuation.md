# C001: Relational individuation

**Status:** proposal (contested)  
**Domain:** ontology / metaphysics / dynamical systems  
**Related:** C002; challenge T001

## Thesis

A genuine individual can be understood as a dynamically maintained, relatively autonomous pattern arising within relations, rather than as an independently self-subsisting substance. Its reality need not depend on its parts staying identical over time.

## Intuition

An eddy in flowing water is more than a list of the molecules temporarily flowing through it. Its coherence depends on exchanges with its surroundings; its boundaries change; and it redirects subsequent flow. It is neither an illusion nor an absolutely separate thing.

**Do not reify the stream.** The environment is itself an evolving network of processes, not an ontologically frozen background.

## Explanatory promise

The approach unifies attention to stability and change, autonomy and dependence, individuality and constitutive relationships.

## Strongest objection

A contemporary substance ontology or structural realism may acknowledge all the same dynamical facts. Why give *process* metaphysical priority rather than treating process as what substances do? See [T001](../challenges/T001-priority-of-process.md).

## What would change the view?

**Research refinement (0.3.0):** every application should specify what is maintained, which processes maintain it, its relevant timescale and boundary, and a strong comparison account. Relational dependence alone does not identify an individual. [P002 develops this requirement](../papers/P002-relational-individuality.md).

An adequate alternative ontology providing equal or better explanations of persistence, agency, and relational constitution with fewer contestable assumptions, or a demonstration that process priority introduces distinctive contradictions.

## Related intellectual trajectories

Whitehead (creativity and concrescence), Simondon (individuation), dynamical systems and autopoiesis. These connections are research directions, not claims that the authors share a single identical doctrine.
