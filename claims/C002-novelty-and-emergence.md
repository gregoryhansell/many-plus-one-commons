# C002: Novelty and emergence

**Status:** proposal (contested)  
**Domain:** metaphysics / philosophy of science  
**Related:** C001, C003

## Thesis

When new patterns of organization arise, they may become causally significant constraints or enabling conditions for subsequent processes. The world acquires new, historically contingent modes of organization.

## Necessary distinctions

1. **Novelty relative to a description or observer** is not by itself ontological novelty.
2. **Weak emergence** may denote complex outcomes derivable in principle but difficult to predict in practice.
3. **Strong emergence**, understood as the emergence of irreducible new causal powers, is a substantive and controversial metaphysical thesis, **not** established merely by observing unexpected system behavior.
4. **Downward causation** has several interpretations, from higher-level constraints to more demanding metaphysical claims; treat these separately.

## Why it matters

**Executed example:** [E001](../experiments/E001-causal-scale/README.md) produces a macro advantage under uniform interventions at each scale, then removes that advantage by matching the intervention distributions in this four-state model. This is a methodological check, not a general refutation or confirmation of strong emergence. Report intervention assumptions whenever using causal-information comparisons.

A non-closed ontology should distinguish newness that changes causal possibility from mere surprise due to incomplete models.

## Strongest objection

Reductionist or mechanistic accounts may explain apparently emergent effects without additional basic causal powers. The project must identify what its richer interpretation explains that disciplined multilevel science does not.

## What would change the view?

**Research refinement (0.3.0):** distinguish descriptive usefulness, interventionally informative organization within a specified model, and constitutive significance for an individual's persistence. Success on one does not establish the others or strong emergence. [P002 develops the comparison](../papers/P002-relational-individuality.md).

Successful cross-level mechanistic accounts may narrow a claim of robust irreducibility. Conversely, well-argued failures of current reductions may motivate further analysis, but are not sufficient proof of strong emergence.

## Revision in edition 0.3.3

[P005](../papers/P005-matched-interventions.md) establishes that deterministic grouping cannot increase total mutual information under the same induced joint experiment, for stochastic as well as deterministic kernels. E001-S checks 16,640 finite cases. This narrows the interpretation of causal-information gains without denying practical macro-level explanation or extending the result to other causal measures.
