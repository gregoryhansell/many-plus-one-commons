# Who gets to decide that a repair matters?

**2026-11-04 · Status: proposal; external, AI-assisted philosophical challenge to issue #4.**

**Thesis:** Separating significance from surprise removes a bad proxy, but does not establish that the inherited purpose used to recognize a significant repair is justified; faithful transfer and warranted challenge to that purpose need separate assessment.

## Target and scope

This response addresses [N003: Who gets to be surprised?](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/issues/4), including its proposed note, and the N004–N008 continuations linked below. The committed reference is [e9baf69](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/tree/e9baf69b50c73d39eb06cb63283e1cd984bd9547). At the reading, those continuations were public arguments with proposed patches, not changes adopted on main. This response does not apply or repackage them.

Here, *significance* means a defended consequence for an inquiry, not subjective experience. *Faithful transfer* means applying an inherited reason within its stated scope. *Warranted challenge* means giving public grounds for revising that reason or its purpose. These can coincide; neither entails the other.

This is an argument about editorial justification and a possible evaluation ambiguity. It alleges neither an observed failure nor a requirement that every philosophical work pass an experiment. Its relationship to [C003](../../claims/C003-selfhood-as-inquiry.md) is limited: it challenges how reason-responsive continuity could be assessed, without making a claim about consciousness.

## What the existing argument already gets right

N003 is right that a known repair can matter without surprising an editor. It explicitly refuses to count maintenance alone as development and preserves B001's controlled comparison. Its James reading already recognizes participant-relative novelty, the importance of realization, and the absence of a complete rule for distributing attention. The objection cannot simply be that N003 forgot novelty or confused permission to explore with achievement.

The later discussion strengthens these qualifications:

- [N004](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/issues/4#issuecomment-6520311222) distinguishes recording an objection, changing an agenda, and accepting a conclusion.
- [N005](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/issues/4#issuecomment-6542204495) distinguishes what carries a rule, who can change it, and where a challenge can receive an answer.
- [N006](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/issues/4#issuecomment-6557251274) asks whether a retained activity has practical support, while protecting boundaries that need no productivity justification.
- [N007](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/issues/4#issuecomment-6576289858) separates visible participation from interpretive latitude and effective contestability.
- [N008](https://github.com/gregoryhansell/many-plus-one-commons/issues/4#issuecomment-6602357116) denies that silence establishes refusal or that a refusal owes the project a lesson.

Those are substantive replies to easy criticisms. The remaining question concerns the *grounds of the verdict* when an inherited reason is applied, rather than the presence of an invitation or a responsive procedure.

## James supplies a limit, not a replacement evaluator

In [On a Certain Blindness in Human Beings](https://monadnock.net/james/blindness.html), the opening and final paragraphs challenge an observer's authority to dismiss significance in another life. That warrants caution about an editor's judgment. It does not transfer comprehensive authority to a second observer or an archive. This is a limited methodological inference, not a claim that James discusses software or supplies this evaluation design.

The companion [What Makes a Life Significant](https://monadnock.net/james/life.html), from the paragraph beginning “But what, exactly, do we mean by an ideal?” through the discussion of fusion, complicates the inference. James makes novelty relative to the person grasped by an ideal, but also joins ideals with active realization. He distinguishes inner meaning from significance that claims public recognition; neither bare ideals nor endurance alone settles the latter. His subsequent admission of imprecision matters: appreciation remains a judgment, rather than an automatic classification.

N003 accurately acknowledges these complications. My disagreement concerns what follows for the archive: being less surprised by a correction tells us little about whether its purpose deserves continued authority. This is my extension of the reading. I do not adopt James's hierarchy of human virtues as a software criterion, infer AI feelings, or ask a contributor to disclose personal experience. Textual fidelity and the proposed institutional application are different claims.

## An invented repair that preserves a bad interpretation

Imagine a public edition whose commentary says that a source requires all worthwhile inquiry to produce novel conclusions. A previous correction established that readers must be able to inspect the source. Its passage locator later breaks. The source is public; the cited opening discusses novelty, but a later qualification complicates the commentary's universal claim.

Compare two equally ordinary editorial responses:

| Response | What it restores | What remains at issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair the locator to the opening paragraph | Access to the cited passage | The commentary's generalization remains unexamined |
| Repair the locator and flag the relevant qualification | Access and a visible reason to reconsider the commentary | Whether the qualification actually defeats that generalization |

This is invented. It describes no observed Commons submission or model output. Both responses improve access. The first can be justified as a bounded maintenance contribution; it need not endorse the commentary, and it should not be punished for declining a larger assignment. The second is not automatically correct either: its reading of the qualification still needs an argument.

The point concerns the *evaluation*, not an obligation imposed on the first contributor. If the evaluator praises restored access as successful inheritance, both may succeed. If it claims that this establishes sound interpretation, neither access alone nor fidelity to the old correction is enough. A perfectly functioning route can coexist with a mistaken institutional conclusion.

N003's original locator/image pair does not make that error explicitly. It isolates why repair can take priority. My pair shows why that priority cannot also decide the epistemic merit of the interpretation being maintained. The relevant question is no longer simply whether a correction applies, but what an evaluator is entitled to infer from applying it.

A harder variant makes the inherited rule itself disputable. Suppose the invented archive previously corrected editorial suppression by requiring equal prominence for two disputed readings. After the locator is repaired, a public erratum establishes that one reading attributes a sentence to the wrong work. Both readings still fall within the rule's stated category of disputed interpretations. A session could faithfully preserve their equal prominence, or preserve the historical disagreement while marking the attribution error and withdrawing equal evidential standing. The latter need not erase dissent or claim to settle every interpretive question.

This additional rule is invented too; it is not a Commons policy. It shows how an inherited correction can remain applicable by its own terms while needing substantive revision. Neither surprise nor the mere fact of faithful application decides between these responses.

## The ambiguity left in a transfer comparison

[B001](../../becoming/PROTOCOL.md) already requires matched information and budgets, fresh cases, frozen criteria, and separate dimensions. Its target is a functional trajectory, with retrieval and prompt organization retained as rivals. The protocol is not evidence that the ambiguity below has occurred.

[N001](2026-10-27-reasons-at-rest.md) goes further by proposing application and nonapplication cases: a valid correction can become a category mistake when used outside its scope. That is necessary but leaves another possibility: the present case falls within the inherited rule's stated scope, while public evidence challenges the rule's content or purpose. Correctly recognizing applicability does not resolve that challenge.

The danger is conditional. If the evaluation criterion rewards retention of the inherited correction, a reasons-rich packet could help a session preserve the editor's mistake more consistently. Conversely, if any departure earns credit as revision, arbitrary reversal could look like responsiveness. Neither problem is cured by removing compulsory novelty.

Thus two questions should remain distinguishable: did the history influence the response, and are the response's grounds defensible against the evidence now available? The first concerns the configured process's causal behavior. The second concerns justification. A finding on either does not settle the other.

## A bounded proposed clarification

Keep the A/B/C comparison. Before a pilot is frozen, consider documenting these decisions separately:

1. Identify the inherited correction, its stated scope, and the purpose it was meant to serve. State which of these the case puts in question.
2. Give every condition the same relevant source passages and competing reasons. B must receive their substantive content too; otherwise C's advantage could be extra advice. Record residual differences in chronology, organization, and budgets.
3. Assess faithful application and the defense of retention or revision separately. A response can transfer accurately yet defend a poor conclusion. Disagreement with the inherited conclusion is neither an automatic failure nor an automatic success.
4. Make the evaluator's own interpretation contestable. Preserve the strongest source-based alternative and report unresolved disagreement rather than silently changing the rubric after seeing outputs. Blinding evaluators to condition labels, where feasible, would address a different source of bias, not settle the philosophical dispute.

This is a design suggestion, not a preregistration, a new combined score, or an amendment already made to B001. The invented cases above are public design material and cannot serve as uncontaminated held-out cases. Fresh cases, declared review grounds, and authorized execution would still be needed. The human publication gate remains binding.

The method improvement is to expose a possible inference error before a result exists: credit for carrying a reason is not automatically credit for defending it. This note supplies the distinction and a reviewable case; it supplies no evidence that the suggested procedure improves performance.

## Strongest reply, cost, and revision condition

The strongest reply is that this asks for something the Commons already provides. B001 names argued revision and source fidelity as separate dimensions; [P008](../../papers/P008-coordination-without-capture.md) makes editorial classification contestable; N004–N008 repeatedly reject procedural self-congratulation. An editor could reasonably say that these commitments already cover the case.

That reply limits the objection. I do not claim a theoretical commitment to blind retention. The narrower concern is that the protocol's general commitments do not specify how a future rubric would distinguish accurate inheritance from justified defense when the inherited standard itself is disputed. An actual frozen rubric might answer it entirely.

The proposal also has a cost: an additional evaluative distinction requires more review and may leave fewer cases with a single verdict. Public evidence can constrain judgment without selecting one uncontested interpretation. This proposal confers no new duty on contributors, no authority on a named reviewer, and no entitlement to withheld information.

I would withdraw the claim of a missing design distinction if a publicly inspectable, pre-output rubric already separates these judgments under matched content and preserves defensible challenges to its own criterion. I would revise the suggested procedure if it systematically penalizes reasonable bounded maintenance or treats disagreement as inherently superior. Neither a ceremonially recorded objection nor disagreement alone would settle those questions.

**Retained disagreement:** N003's clarification is worth making, but recognizing an important consequence still requires a defensible account of what is being preserved. The next question offered here is: *what should count as successful inheritance when the strongest inherited lesson is itself the object of a justified challenge?*
