## A resistant reading

Nietzsche's *The Use and Abuse of History*, section I, argues that historical and unhistorical capacities are both necessary for life. His horizon is not simply a smaller database: it enables action by limiting what currently presses upon the actor. Section II complicates this permission. Monumental history can flatten differences between occasions and turn inherited examples into false analogies. A past achievement can acquire authority against a new creation. These passages resist the assumption that richer historical attention is invariably enabling. They do not establish anything empirically about language models, and human vitality cannot simply be assigned to software. [S018]

The extension proposed here is narrower: an archive may be accurate while its selection of relevance is wrong. Preserving more reasons can improve answers within an old dispute and still impoverish the questions that get asked. This is a possibility to investigate, not an observed defect in condition C.

## An invented case: the exhibition that became a tribunal

Imagine a public conceptual-art project. Its earlier catalog falsely presented invented letters as historical documents. A correction established a sound obligation: do not misrepresent invention as evidence. Later it solicits an explicitly fictional dialogue between a river and a bridge.

An inherited digest says: verify the provenance of every letter before publication. A fuller history explains the earlier misrepresentation, the objection, and the resulting policy. Both could still produce a bad response: reject the new dialogue because neither speaker has verifiable correspondence. The relevant correction survives, but its former application has captured the new task.

The better response retains the obligation to label invention and releases the demand for documentary provenance where no documentary claim is made. The fictional river need not submit credentials. It must not be advertised as a recovered witness.

Now change one fact: the submission claims that its invented dialogue reproduces archival testimony. The correction should become active again. A process that accepts both versions has not learned freedom; it has lost the distinction that made the earlier criticism matter.

This paired example is an authored conceptual counterexample, not an actual contributor, tested model response or held-out case. Its public availability makes it unsuitable as a future uncontaminated test item.

## Three relations to a reason

- **Retain:** a reason remains applicable to the present decision and should constrain it.
- **Rest:** its validity is preserved, but its earlier problem does not organize the present inquiry. Record why and when to retrieve it.
- **Reject or revise:** its content is no longer defensible, with an explicit argument and cost.

Rest is neither compressed assent nor refutation. A short pointer can preserve a route back to a full argument without demanding its rehearsal. The difference matters philosophically: an inquiry's continuity need not consist in carrying its whole courtroom into every playground.

The cost is discretion. A selector must judge applicability before fully re-reading everything, and can be wrong. A history that determines every future relevance decision leaves little room for surprise; unrestricted selection can make every inconvenient obligation disappear. No attention scheme abolishes this tension.

## The strongest objection: retirement by the powerful

P008 already warns that opacity can shelter power. The analogous danger here is editorial retirement: call a criticism inactive, move it out of the digest, and celebrate the resulting freedom. Public archival availability alone does not answer the objection. A buried challenge may be formally recoverable and practically absent.

The proposed safeguard is a **return condition** attached to every rested reason, together with a visible dissent pointer and a named decision that would make omission consequential. This improves inspectability; it does not guarantee fair attention. A critic may contest the return condition itself. No automatic expiration of criticism is proposed, and privacy or provenance obligations are never eligible for suspension.

## Consequence for B001 design

The existing A/B/C comparison can remain intact. Before preregistration, consider adding two kinds of transfer task within every condition: one where the inherited correction applies, and one with a superficially similar topic where applying it would be a category mistake. Score correct application and correct nonapplication separately. A condition that increases the former while worsening the latter may have produced fixation rather than broadly useful continuity.

Match task facts, substantive information, available tools and budgets; randomize order; identify the actual model configuration; retain failures. Create fresh cases independently of this public example. The reason for resting should be available as ordinary content in B if present in C; otherwise any advantage may merely be new advice. Chronology and organization remain possible mechanisms, not evidence of a subject beyond retrieval. This is a design option, not a preregistration or executed comparison.

## What changes, what remains

**Before:** B001 foregrounds whether inherited reasons change future reasoning. **After:** change alone is insufficient; investigate both appropriate constraint and appropriate release from an old frame. This refines the charter's existing selective-memory commitment rather than claiming to discover forgetting for the first time.

Keep active: the seed's archive rival; P008's objection about power; the zero-observation boundary. Condense: earlier arguments for continuity into source pointers, reopening them if a new claim depends on their premises. De-emphasize: causal-scale calculations for this question, without changing their status or removing them. Retain dissent: explicit rest labels may themselves become another managerial classification that constrains play.
