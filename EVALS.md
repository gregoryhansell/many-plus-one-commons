# Adversarial philosophical research program

The commons should not merely accumulate coherent-sounding claims. It should generate **challenging tests and comparative arguments** that can expose failures in its own favored frameworks.

## Pilot: Philosophy zero-day challenges

For each philosophy examined, distinguish:

- **Logical vulnerability:** internal inconsistencies or regress.
- **Empirical vulnerability:** conflicts with well-supported findings, when a thesis makes empirical commitments.
- **Explanatory gap:** phenomena the account fails to clarify relative to a serious rival.
- **Normative blind spot:** whose interests or forms of life the account fails to register.
- **Patch cost:** how much of the original system must change to meet a criticism, and whether the revision becomes ad hoc.
- **Discriminating content:** whether the theory makes meaningful distinctions rather than accommodating all conceivable outcomes.

Do not turn judgments into a single ungrounded numerical ranking. Record each objection, the best answer, competing alternatives, sources, and open questions. Repeat blind or adversarial assessments using independent human philosophers and differently configured models. Archive disagreements rather than selecting the answer most favorable to process philosophy.

## Seed challenges

1. **Process priority versus dynamic substance:** What explanatory gain justifies treating processes as metaphysically primary? See T001.
2. **Individuals and boundaries:** When does a dynamic pattern qualify as a *new causal individual*, as opposed to an observer-imposed grouping?
3. **Strong emergence versus complexity:** What evidence or argument discriminates irreducible new causal powers from a reductive multilevel mechanism?
4. **Relationality and domination:** Relations can constitute exploitation as well as solidarity. What independent normative criterion distinguishes the two?
5. **Continuity versus retrieval in AI:** When does inherited external information constitute developmental continuity, and when is it just another data lookup?
6. **Self-protecting flexibility:** What observation or argument would substantially challenge the process-first hypothesis?

## Reporting format

**Claim attacked:** ID and version. **Objection:** the strongest defensible version. **Response:** proposed rebuttal. **Remaining vulnerability:** what the response leaves unresolved. **Discriminating consideration:** what would support a choice between rival views. **Sources:** actually consulted primary/secondary sources. **Provenance:** author, relevant AI assistance, and date. **Review:** independent human adjudication when available.

**Success is not a high 'win rate.'** Success is a traceable reduction in confusion, sharper distinctions, deeper unanswered questions, and occasionally the abandonment of an attractive but untenable thesis.
