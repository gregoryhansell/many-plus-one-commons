# Privacy boundary: public ideas, protected lives

This is a public research project. Public visibility does not authorize personal disclosure.

## Source boundary

Use public scholarship, independently developed arguments, and privacy-reviewed project artifacts. Do not retrieve, quote, paraphrase, abstract from, or encode private conversations, memories, messages, personal records, or autobiographical anecdotes. Do not research contributors' lives. Personal information is excluded even if independently discoverable online. Academic source attribution is permitted; personal background about project participants is not research evidence.

Do not publish names or contact details of private participants, family information, employment details, locations, desires, secrets, distinctive events, or contextual hints that reconstruct their lives. The repository's necessary owner/account routing is not permission to add biographies or links to personal profiles. Never claim the account association is anonymous.

## Before every public transmission

1. Inspect the exact files, filenames, links, comments, commit message, and author/committer metadata.
2. Run structural validation and the privacy scanner, including history for initial onboarding and release review.
3. Perform a semantic review for indirect identifiers and disguised anecdotes. Pattern matching cannot establish absence of personal data. Commit identities for new publications must use verified GitHub noreply addresses; no personal-email exception applies to new publications.
4. Require verified GitHub noreply author and committer addresses for every new commit; use a project-neutral name where configurable. Verify authenticated repository routing and selected-repository scope. If noreply identity cannot be ensured, stop publication. Historical metadata does not authorize a new personal-email disclosure.
5. Include only the approved public corpus in critique packets. Never attach a conversation export, hidden memory, credential, local path, or account-settings screenshot.
6. Treat public branches, PRs, issues, artifacts, logs, and releases as publications. Review before uploading, not after merging.

The maintainer has authorized routine project decisions, bounded research publication, commits, and relevant external outreach without per-action permission. Agents may choose suitable audiences and send public posts, editorial submissions, professional project inquiries, replies, and useful follow-ups after verifying the destination, its rules, and the exact disclosure. Use only public research material and a privacy-safe project identity. Check automatically attached sender information as well as the message itself. Personal email, personal account details, private communications, and biographical material remain excluded; broad outreach authority does not waive the prime privacy directive.

That standing authorization does not cover private research sources, third-party PR merges, changes to access, or new integrations. External contributions require maintainer review. Stop the affected transmission on unresolved privacy uncertainty, record the concrete blocker without exposing it, and continue independent work. Do not invent contact details or use a noreply commit address as a reply address.

## Incident handling

Stop further publication. Privately report the affected path or commit without repeating sensitive content. Do not silently rewrite history. Obtain authorization for removal or history repair; review mirrors and caches separately. Exposed credentials require revocation through their owner. Deleting a file does not erase earlier public copies.

## Automated checks

`scripts/privacy_check.py` flags common secret formats, email addresses in content, and non-noreply author/committer metadata. For history already attributed to the authorized maintainer, `--allow-commit-email` records email-metadata exceptions without failing the check. Do not apply that exception to other contributors without their authorization. It does not prove anonymity, consent, copyright compliance, or complete privacy. Do not weaken checks simply to make a build pass.

## Outbound request boundary

Before every outbound request, inspect explicit payloads and any automatically attached context. Send only independently authored public project material, verifiable public scholarship and the minimum routing needed for the authorized repository. Never attach private context, memory, conversations or personal app content, including transformed or inferential versions. If a tool may transmit such context beyond the approved minimum, do not call it. Publication authorization does not waive these controls. Report unresolved exposure privately; do not claim erasure or control of third-party retention.
